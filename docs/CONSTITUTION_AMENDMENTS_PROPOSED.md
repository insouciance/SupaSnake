# Amendment Package A1 — The Living-Game Review

**Status:** RATIFIED by owner direction, 25 July 2026 ("yeah that sounds better"),
with A8 (the Acquisition Engine) added on the owner's follow-up directive and the
cooling period waived by direct engagement. Folded into **Constitution v1.1** — this
file is now the amendment record, not the live text.
**Version reviewed:** Constitution v1.0, 25 July 2026.
**Charge:** the owner's directive to review the Constitution as Executive Product
Director and Business Director — find where it overcorrected toward protection at the
expense of engagement and commercial success; critique the owner's position where it
is wrong; defend the Constitution where it is stronger; propose targeted amendments.

**Numbers remain hypotheses [H]** with tests, per the Constitution's own standard.

---

## Part I — Verdict on the owner's position

The charge is substantially correct in four places, and wrong in three. Both halves
matter, so both are argued.

### Where the owner is right

**R1 — The Constitution treats revenue as a byproduct, and a company cannot.** §10.7
honestly concedes "below a few thousand retained players this funds hosting, not
salary" — and then offers no plan to change that number. That is fatalism dressed as
honesty. A ten-year live service needs revenue instrumentation, targets, unit
economics, and a plan for the day revenue should fund headcount and acquisition. The
Constitution has none of this. It also quietly assumed "one developer, five years" is
a permanent identity rather than what it actually is: a description of current
operating capacity that revenue is allowed to change. (Amendments A4, A2, A3.)

**R2 — The competitive spine is incomplete.** The owner's two-year Survivor.io
testimony named three load-bearing structures: weekly events, a **monthly promotion
league**, and the clan number. The Constitution built the weekly (Serpent) and the
daily (Signal) and deliberately skipped the league, citing small-N caution and
Duolingo's demotion coercion. That skipped the best-documented engagement mechanism
in the entire research corpus (+25% claimed) instead of solving its two real defects
(demotion loss and cohort-size dependency), both of which are solvable. The
individual player currently has no ascending long-term competitive goal — Mastery is
PvE-flavored, the Score ladder is a list, and Depth is measured against yourself. For
a player who wants to *climb*, the Constitution offers a mirror. (Amendment A1.)

**R3 — Marketing-as-product stops at the share card.** Rule 14 and the share grid
are right but *artifact-shaped*: static images with URLs. The Constitution contains
nothing about spectacle — replays, ghosts, clips, creators, spectating a clanmate's
best run. A precision game whose signature is a greed decision under pressure is a
*clip machine*, and the audit's own veteran-needs list ("verified replay or ghost
sharing") flagged it; the Constitution dropped it. The campus strategy also
under-uses creators: a streamer's community is a campus, and the product ships no
tool for them. (Amendment A5.)

**R4 — The superfan ceiling is €24.99, once.** The Constitution's own cited proof
that cosmetic-only monetization works — Path of Exile — runs supporter packs from
$30 to $480, and that ladder is *why* it works. SupaSnake's price architecture stops
where PoE's begins. Refusing consumables and power is the soul; refusing to let a
devoted adult spend €50 on beautiful, permanent, fully-specified patronage is not
ethics, it is a ceiling the Constitution built out of symmetry with its own caution.
(Amendment A2.)

### Where the owner is wrong, and the Constitution should hold

**W1 — "The world moves without you" argues against a rule the Constitution doesn't
have.** Rule 5 does not freeze the world for absent players. Signals happen without
you and archive unplayed; Serpent weeks settle without you; rivals climb; your clan
hunts; seasons turn. What Rule 5 bans is **manufactured loss** — streak resets,
expiring tracks, decaying possessions. The sports analogy, examined closely, is *for*
Rule 5, not against it: the runner who skips a month loses fitness (skill rust is
real and unmanufactured) and loses ground to those who trained (relative motion —
which the amendments below make far more visible). The league does not confiscate
their past medals. Streak resets and expiring passes are medal confiscation. The
correct fix is to make the world's motion *visible and felt* on return (A6), not to
make absence destructive. The distinction the owner draws — "never manufacture
frustration to sell relief" — is exactly the line Rule 5 already draws. The rule
stands; its presentation was underbuilt.

**W2 — "Healthy competitive pressure" has a documented failure tail, and the owner
is its witness.** The same testimony that praised the clan number also said the game
"sometimes feels more like an obligation than entertainment, demanding both time and
money." Pressure systems do not stay healthy by intention; they stay healthy by
structure. Every amendment below that adds pressure (leagues, rivalry memory) is
therefore built promotion-only, threshold-based, and attendance-capped — pressure to
*perform*, never pressure to *attend*. Where the owner's framing drifts toward
rewarding attendance itself, this review declines to follow, on the owner's own
two-year evidence.

**W3 — Paid UA "eventually, when unit economics are healthy" is right — and is
precisely why it cannot be a launch posture.** The studios the owner invokes are the
proof: Supercell soft-launches for months and kills games with weak retention
regardless of revenue potential, and only then buys traffic. Buying users into an
unproven funnel is burning runway to avoid learning the product truth. The
Constitution was right to sequence organic-first; it was wrong to write "not paid
UA" into the *fallback* clause as though the tool were dirty rather than merely
mistimed. A4 sanctions it behind gates: retention proven, LTV measured, payback
bounded. One nuance the Constitution missed in the owner's favor: SupaSnake's
click-to-play web funnel (the ad *is* the game, five seconds from click to board,
no platform tax on conversion) is structurally better UA material than most web
games — a reason to build the gate now rather than never.

### Where the Constitution should also self-correct (not raised by the owner)

**S1 —** §10.7's honest pessimism should have come with the plan that changes the
number (this package is that plan). **S2 —** "No gifting until population gates" was
over-cautious: gifting needs one friend, not a population, and a gift to a
non-player is an acquisition event with a bow on it. **S3 —** The commercial calendar
(four events/year) confused *in-product restraint* (correct, keep) with *commercial
tempo* (wrong: a permanent catalog can still grow monthly, and out-of-product
channels can say so loudly).

## Part II — What is defended, unchanged

For the record, against both the owner's pressure and any future rereading:

1. **Zero consumables; money never touches a computed number (Rules 3–4).**
   Load-bearing, not preference. Depth is build-inclusive *because* build is
   unbuyable; the instant money can reach DNA, variants, genes, or boosts, Depth
   becomes a spending scoreboard and the clan becomes Survivor.io's pressure — the
   exact failure the owner named. The owner's own "no pay-to-win" demand makes this
   non-negotiable in this architecture. Also: OGH 6 Ob 228/24h makes
   indirectly-paid randomness a live legal risk in this jurisdiction. No amendment
   touches these rules, and A2's packs are engineered to stay outside them.
2. **Non-expiring seasons and the never-rotating catalog.** Not just ethics —
   compounding business: expiring passes have zero back-catalog revenue; SupaSnake's
   season shelf sells forever and grows every quarter (Deep Rock precedent). The 2029
   player buying four years of back-seasons is a revenue tail no expiring model has.
3. **Retention before monetization scale-up (§14 phase gates).** This *is* the
   Supercell doctrine the owner invoked. Kept exactly.
4. **In-product commercial sanctity (Rule 7).** The store as a district, zero
   commerce in runs and Results, no commercial notifications. PoE hypes packs
   *outside* the client; the game itself never knocks. A3 raises commercial tempo
   without touching this — the distinction between product and owned media does the
   work.
5. **Rule 8 in full.** No clan mechanic improvable by money, no thresholds, no
   officer levers. Every amendment below was checked against it.

## Part III — The amendments

### A1 — Ascension Leagues: the monthly individual climb

- **Current philosophy:** individual competition = the Score ladder (a you-centered
  list) plus self-referential Depth. Leagues were rejected via Duolingo's demotion
  coercion and cohort-size dependency at small N (§6.1, §9.3).
- **Problem:** no ascending, named, long-term competitive goal for the individual.
  The owner's own retention anatomy (Ender's Echo) had a monthly promotion league as
  a primary login driver; the research corpus's strongest engagement number
  (+25% [R]) sits here; the Constitution offers climbers a mirror instead of a
  mountain.
- **Why it matters:** "reasons to improve" needs a legible altitude. Mastery caps
  out; personal bests are private; Depth is cooperative. A league gives the daily
  Signal *stakes* and gives the month a narrative arc — anticipation with an ending,
  renewed twelve times a year at zero marginal content cost.
- **Proposed replacement:** **Ascension** — a monthly league on Signal performance.
  League points = the sum of your best **K = 10 [H]** daily Signal scores that
  month. Tiers are **absolute published thresholds** at launch (work at N = 1, no
  cohorts needed), converting to percentile bands only when population justifies
  [H, gate]. **Promotion-only within a cycle** — you can never fall mid-month; each
  cycle starts fresh with your history in the Chronicle (seasonal standings, per the
  Constitution's existing framing: standings reset, possessions never). Rewards:
  tier marks, frames, an Apex-tier cosmetic per cycle — earned-only, never sold,
  never economy. Sum-of-best-K is the sports analogy formalized: attendance helps
  up to K days, then only *quality* helps — training is rewarded, attendance is not
  farmable.
- **Expected business impact:** the strongest known daily-return multiplier attached
  to the existing daily surface; lifts the retained population every other revenue
  line scales with. No direct revenue (rewards unsellable by design).
- **Expected player impact:** the daily Signal acquires monthly meaning; climbers
  get a mountain; the Wordle-style share grid gains a league context line.
- **Risks:** a third number in practice (league points) risks Score/Depth conflation
  — mitigated: league points are explicitly presented as "Score, this month" (an
  aggregation view, not a new metric); absolute thresholds may need retuning as the
  skill curve settles (they are [H] and published as such); K too low → grindable,
  too high → attendance pressure (test in §17 extension).
- **Tradeoffs:** cap table line amends from "one daily surface" to "one daily
  surface (Signal, including its monthly Ascension view)" — a real loosening,
  honestly recorded; some players will experience tier thresholds as pressure — that
  is the point, and it is opt-in by ignoring it (Signals play identically without
  looking at Ascension).

### A2 — The Patronage Ladder: seasonal supporter packs

- **Current philosophy:** four SKU archetypes; the high end is Founding Keeper,
  €24.99, once, ever (§10.2).
- **Problem:** the devoted adult fan has nowhere to go. The Constitution's own
  evidence base (PoE, $30–$480 packs, Tencent-validated) shows the trust-compatible
  cosmetic model earns its keep *through the top of its price ladder*, not its
  floor. SupaSnake's ARPPU ceiling is currently set by its cheapest-possible
  reading of its own sources.
- **Why it matters:** revenue scales with retained population *times* the depth of
  the offer to those who love the game most. Refusing power is the soul; refusing
  depth is just leaving the lights off.
- **Proposed replacement:** per season, up to three **Patron Packs** — e.g. €19.99 /
  €49.99 / €99.99 [H] — each a named, fully-specified, permanent cosmetic set plus
  patron tenure marks ("Patron of the Serpent's Year"), under the existing
  Founding/Patronage archetype (SKU archetype count stays 4). Rules, binding:
  contents are pack-specific in kind — never earnable items repackaged, never
  power-adjacent, never randomized; packs never retire (back-catalog forever, like
  everything else); tenure marks remain unbuyable retroactively; no pack is ever
  discounted (no anchor games).
- **Expected business impact:** the single largest legitimate ARPPU lever available
  to this model. [H] If 0.3–0.5% of retained players take a mid-tier pack per
  season, ARPPU plausibly rises 2–4× over the €3.99-sub-only base — the difference
  between "funds hosting" and "funds the roadmap" at identical population.
- **Expected player impact:** free and low-spend players unaffected (nothing new is
  gated); devoted fans get a dignified way to matter financially; patron marks make
  supporting the game part of identity.
- **Risks:** visible high-price SKUs can read as "whale game" if presented with
  urgency or prominence — mitigated by Rule 7 (destination-only), the no-discount
  rule, and framing as patronage; cosmetic production budget rises (packs must be
  *worth* €49.99 in craft — solo art capacity is the real constraint, staged via
  §12.1 slot 4 costs).
- **Tradeoffs:** the "€6.99 ceiling" simplicity story is gone; monetization
  sentiment must be watched as first-class (already mandated §10.6); some press/
  players will criticize any three-digit SKU on principle — accepted, answered by
  the contract (§3).

### A3 — Commercial tempo: monthly catalog growth, gifting, owned media

- **Current philosophy:** four commercial events per year; gifting deferred behind
  clan population gates; Rule 7 read as near-silence about commerce everywhere
  (§10.5, §12.4 of audit heritage).
- **Problem:** in-product restraint was correct and is kept; but the Constitution
  accidentally set the *company's* commercial tempo to quarterly, muted its owned
  media, and postponed the one purchase that is also an acquisition event (a gift).
- **Why it matters:** a permanent catalog that grows monthly gives players a reason
  to *look* without any pressure to hurry — the anti-FOMO version of shop cadence.
  Gifts convert affection into revenue *and* into invitations.
- **Proposed replacement:** (1) the Atelier grows monthly — additions, never
  rotations; prices never move; nothing leaves. (2) **Gifting ships in Phase 3**
  (was: post-gates): any owned-catalog cosmetic, giftable to a friend by handle or
  — the acquisition case — by invite link to a non-player, who receives it on
  signup. No gifting prompts inside clan surfaces, ever (Rule 8 adjacency). (3) A
  clarification appended to Rule 7: it governs the product; **owned media** —
  Discord, socials, an opt-in news list — may carry commercial news loudly. The
  game never knocks; the community channel may shout.
- **Expected business impact:** twelve soft commercial beats a year instead of
  four, at zero urgency cost; gifting adds a revenue line that doubles as referral
  infrastructure with built-in warmth.
- **Expected player impact:** the store becomes a place things *arrive* rather than
  a museum; receiving a gifted cosmetic is a strong first-session moment for
  invited players.
- **Risks:** monthly additions strain solo art capacity (same constraint as A2 —
  the two share a production budget and should share a pipeline); gifting needs
  fraud/refund handling (Stripe-side, bounded).
- **Tradeoffs:** "four events per year" was a beautiful marketing sentence; the
  replacement sentence — "nothing in our shop ever leaves, and something new
  arrives every month" — is better.

### A4 — The business layer: instrumentation, targets, UA gate, team-scaling

- **Current philosophy:** §10.6 mandates trust-side metrics and explicitly refuses
  engagement-extraction telemetry; §10.7 concedes small revenue and counsels
  patience; the fallback clause forbids paid UA outright; "one developer" is
  treated as a constant.
- **Problem:** SupaSnake is also a company, and the Constitution gave the company
  no dashboard, no targets, no acquisition doctrine, and no growth path for its own
  operating capacity.
- **Why it matters:** ten years of live operation are funded or they don't happen.
  Revenue being *designed for* is not the same as revenue being *extracted* — the
  Constitution conflated the two by omission.
- **Proposed replacement:** a new Constitution section, "The Company":
  (1) **Business KPIs**, reviewed quarterly alongside the product north star: MRR,
  ARPPU, conversion, subscription tenure, refund/cancel rates, LTV by cohort,
  monetization sentiment — and *never* per-session revenue or offer impressions
  (§10.6's refusal stands verbatim). (2) **Milestone bands** tying revenue to
  capacity, owner-set [H]: hosting-covered → part-time sustainable → full-time
  sustainable → second hire → UA budget. (3) **The paid-UA gate:** sanctioned when
  W4 retention ≥ target [H], LTV measured on ≥2 cohorts, and blended payback ≤ 6
  months [H]; capped at a fixed % of trailing revenue; forbidden before
  retention-proof (buying traffic to discover retention is prohibited — the
  soft-launch doctrine). The click-to-play funnel advantage (ad → live board in
  seconds, no platform tax) is noted as SupaSnake's structural UA edge. (4) **The
  team-scaling clause** (owner-confirmed direction: growing the company is a goal
  of the plan, not an exception to it): every cap in §12 is classified on
  ratification as either **capacity-bound** — it exists because one person runs
  the game today, and relaxes by amendment as headcount grows (content cadence,
  authored world events, cosmetic production volume, moderation surface, support
  depth, LLM features) — or **identity-bound** — it exists because of what
  SupaSnake is, and holds at any team size (one currency, two numbers, three
  pillars, no consumables, no manufactured loss, no paid power, commerce in its
  district). The solo constraint is a phase, not the game's identity; the
  milestone bands above are the ladder out of it. A 2030 team of five inherits
  the identity-bound caps exactly as written — headcount buys cadence, never a
  license for mode sprawl.
- **Expected business impact:** the company can steer: targets exist, UA unlocks at
  the right moment instead of never, and success has a defined shape beyond
  survival.
- **Expected player impact:** none directly — and that is the point: the business
  layer is instrumentation and doctrine, not new player-facing pressure.
- **Risks:** targets can quietly become the product (the audit's "systems whose
  primary purpose is increasing DAU" failure) — mitigated by keeping the forbidden-
  telemetry list and the quarterly review's fixed agenda: trust metrics are read
  *before* revenue metrics, in that order, every time.
- **Tradeoffs:** the document loses the purity of "revenue is a byproduct"; it
  gains the honesty of "revenue is a goal with rules." The fallback clause's "not
  paid UA" is struck and replaced by the gate.

### A5 — The spectacle layer: replays, ghosts, challenge links, creators

- **Current philosophy:** sharing = artifacts (grids, cards, URLs); Rule 14;
  campus seeding via invites (§11). No replays, no ghosts, no clips, no creator
  tooling, no spectating.
- **Problem:** the Constitution markets the game's *results* but not its *drama*.
  The portal decision under pressure — the INFUSE at length 9, the greedy PASS that
  dies two foods later — is the most watchable thing SupaSnake produces, and
  nothing captures it. The audit flagged replay/ghost sharing for veterans; the
  marketing section never picked it up.
- **Why it matters:** clips are the only marketing that scales itself; creators are
  campuses with distribution built in; ghosts deepen the clan-witness mechanic
  (watching a clanmate's record run *is* the witness function, animated); and
  challenge links turn every share from a picture into a playable dare.
- **Proposed replacement:** staged spectacle infrastructure. (1) **Deterministic
  replays** (input-log + seed + content version — the engine is already seeded and
  server-recomputed; determinism is a formalization, not a rewrite [M–L cost,
  honestly]): every notable run (PB, world-first, Serpent best) stores its log.
  (2) **Ghost racing:** run today's Signal against the translucent ghost of a
  clanmate's or rival's attempt. (3) **Challenge links:** every Signal share URL is
  playable — "beat my 1,240 on Signal #214" drops the visitor onto the same seed,
  ghost optional. The share stops being a screenshot and becomes a duel. (4)
  **Clip export:** one-tap replay-to-video for the run's final 15 seconds at PB/
  record moments (the game detects its own highlights — world-firsts already
  exist). (5) **Creator seeds** (Phase 4): named custom Signal conditions a creator
  can issue to their community — a campus-seeding tool that costs the developer a
  config row. (6) The proposal template (§16 checklist) gains one question: *what
  is this feature's shareable moment, if any?* — an evaluation lens, deliberately
  not a dilution-test gate (features must be allowed to be quietly excellent; a
  mandatory share angle breeds gimmicks — the audit's DAU-first failure mode).
- **Expected business impact:** the acquisition thesis gains its missing scalable
  channel (clips/creators) at near-zero marginal cost per share; challenge links
  measurably tighten the share→play funnel (the visitor lands *in a game*, not on
  a picture).
- **Expected player impact:** competition becomes visible and social at every
  scale; the clan witness deepens from numbers to watching; veterans get an
  audience.
- **Risks:** replay determinism across content versions is real engineering and
  must not slip before Phase 1's foundations (it is Phase 3–4 work); ghost
  visibility needs privacy defaults (opt-out, clan-scoped by default).
- **Tradeoffs:** this is the package's largest engineering line-item — it is also
  the only amendment that creates a new *kind* of asset (watchable SupaSnake), and
  it is staged so nothing earlier depends on it.

### A6 — The world that moved: the return experience

- **Current philosophy:** "returning after a long absence produces a summary, not a
  claim cascade" — deferred as a possible later feature (kill list #7 note).
- **Problem:** the Constitution defends absence flawlessly but renders return
  flatly. The owner's instinct is right: a living game should *show* that it lived.
- **Why it matters:** the return moment is the single highest-leverage retention
  moment a lapsed player has, and the difference between "nothing happened without
  you" (dead world) and "look what happened while you were away" (living world) is
  presentation, not mechanics — the mechanics already exist.
- **Proposed replacement:** the **World Report** ships at launch (promoted from
  deferred): on return after ≥3 absent days [H], one screen — never blocking Launch
  (Rule 1, ≤2 taps preserved) — leading with motion, not loss: Serpent weeks that
  settled and how your clan fared; your rival's Depth delta; Ascension movement;
  what the current Signal is. Written as news ("HOLLOW FANG reached Depth 51,000
  without you — they left the door open"), never as debt (no claims, no catch-up
  tasks, nothing owed). Tone rule, binding: the Report may make a player *miss*
  the world; it may never make them *owe* it.
- **Expected business impact:** directly targets lapsed-player reactivation — the
  cheapest population there is; gives the (opt-in) settlement email its natural
  deep-link target.
- **Expected player impact:** absence feels like missing a match day, not like
  returning to a parking meter; the owner's "world moved on" feeling delivered
  inside Rule 5, not against it.
- **Risks:** tone drift toward guilt over time — the binding tone rule and Rule 5
  reviewer question guard it; one more surface to maintain (small, templated).
- **Tradeoffs:** kill-list entry #7's "may return later" hedge becomes a
  commitment, spending Phase-1-adjacent effort on presentation.

### A7 — Rivalry memory: persistent rivals, head-to-head history

- **Current philosophy:** Serpent rival pairing is memoryless — banded matchmaking
  per week, no continuity (§9.4).
- **Problem:** anonymous, re-rolled opposition cannot become a story. Sports
  leagues run on derbies: the *same* opponent, again, with history. The
  Constitution built symmetric pairing and forgot to let it accumulate.
- **Why it matters:** clan identity ("strong clan identity" — the owner's explicit
  ask) is cheap to deepen here: a named rival with a head-to-head record is
  narrative infrastructure that writes itself weekly.
- **Proposed replacement:** pairing prefers the standing rival while both clans
  remain in-band [H]: head-to-head records (W–L, streaks, closest week, all-time
  margin) live in both clans' Chronicles; a season-end **Rivalry Ledger** entry
  memorializes the year's duels. Re-pairing occurs on sustained band divergence
  (walkover protection unchanged and still primary). Rewards unchanged — heraldic
  laurels only (Rule 8 untouched).
- **Expected business impact:** none direct; retention via narrative — rivalries
  are the cheapest recurring content in sports for a reason.
- **Expected player impact:** "them again" — the week acquires a face; settlement
  cards become trash-talk material (share loop compounds).
- **Risks:** a persistently losing clan needs an honorable exit — mitigated: either
  clan may decline continuation at season boundaries, silently, no forfeit
  recorded; band divergence auto-dissolves mismatches.
- **Tradeoffs:** slightly less optimal weekly symmetry in exchange for continuity;
  a small state surface (rival edges) added to clan data.

### A8 — The Acquisition Engine: the funnel as a designed product surface

- **Current philosophy:** §11 v1.0 has positioning, three organic loops, the campus
  strategy, and web hygiene — principles and artifacts, but no end-to-end funnel, no
  channel portfolio, no lead-capture mechanism, no operating rhythm, and no answer
  to "who does marketing, when, with what hours."
- **Problem:** "how players arrive" was a thesis, not a machine. A thesis cannot be
  instrumented, reviewed weekly, or improved. The owner's directive is correct:
  acquisition must be designed into the product with a clear mechanism and plan from
  the start, not appended after launch.
- **Why it matters:** every other amendment scales with arrivals. Ascension, packs,
  gifting, and the Serpent all multiply a population that must first exist. A
  solo-operated game's acquisition must be mostly *structural* — built into surfaces
  that sell the game while the developer sleeps — because operator hours are the
  scarcest resource in the company.
- **Proposed replacement:** a full Acquisition Engine written into §11:
  (1) **The funnel, eight named stages** — Reach → Arrive → Activate → Identify →
  Habituate → Belong → Advocate → Patronize — each with its mechanism, its metric,
  and its owning surface, instrumented from Phase 0. The activation event is defined
  (first BANKED extraction [H]) so onboarding optimizes toward the game's thesis
  landing, not toward generic completion. (2) **The lead ladder:** anonymous play is
  never gated; *public identity* is the conversion mechanism — leaderboards show
  provisional unclaimed entries, and claiming a handle (free) is required to enroll
  in Ascension, found or join a clan, and sign shared artifacts; an optional email
  at claim attaches the player to the settlement Dispatch. Pre-launch, the landing
  page carries a one-field Dispatch waitlist so spike traffic is captured before the
  habit surfaces exist. (3) **The Snake Query Engine:** "snake game" is one of the
  largest evergreen casual-game search families on the web, and SupaSnake is
  structurally the best destination such a query can land on — click to live board
  in seconds, no install. An honest intent surface (fast logged-out landing, a
  /play page, structured data, sitemap, and the Rule 14 artifact-URL long tail)
  compounds in the background at near-zero marginal cost. (4) **Broodmarks:** a
  two-sided referral — inviter and invitee earn a permanent linked cosmetic mark and
  a Chronicle brood-kin record when the invitee *activates* [H] — never currency,
  never power, inert to self-referral farming by the activation gate. (5) **The
  product-generated content engine:** the game writes its own marketing — weekly
  Serpent settlements auto-compose into a shareable post (top clans, record Depths,
  world-firsts) that the operator publishes with one tap. (6) **The operating
  rhythm, sized for one person:** weekly ≤1h (publish the settlement post, one
  community touch, read the funnel dashboard); monthly ~half-day (Atelier addition
  + devlog); quarterly (season reveal, patron packs, press ping, channel review).
  Marketing is never zero and never the job: floor = the weekly ritual, ceiling
  ≈ 15% of operator time [H]. (7) **The manifesto asset:** the player contract (§3)
  is published at a linkable URL as the press hook — the one story competitors
  cannot run.
- **Expected business impact:** the growth loop stops depending on operator
  willpower: SEO, artifact URLs, challenge links, Broodmarks, and the auto-composed
  weekly post all work unattended; funnel instrumentation makes campus results
  readable and makes the §10.8 paid-UA gate *reachable* (no LTV/CAC discipline is
  possible without stage metrics).
- **Expected player impact:** none coercive — anonymous play stays unlimited; the
  conversion asks are real reasons (public identity, continuity, the Dispatch), at
  designed moments, always declinable.
- **Risks:** SEO is a years-long channel against entrenched results (treated as
  compounding background, measured quarterly, never spam); provisional-entry claiming
  needs anti-impersonation care (claim windows, device continuity); the operating
  rhythm competes with dev hours — hence the explicit floor and ceiling.
- **Tradeoffs:** §11 grows from a thesis into a machine, which is more to maintain;
  the alternative — a beautiful game nobody finds — is the one failure no other
  amendment can compensate for.

## Part V — The one-paragraph synthesis

The Constitution built a game that cannot betray its players; this package makes it
a game that can also *afford* them. The soul — two honest numbers, a witness-sized
clan, commerce that never knocks, absence without punishment — survives every
amendment untouched, because each amendment adds pull (a mountain to climb, a rival
to beat, a world that visibly moved, a way for love to become patronage) and none
adds push. That is the distinction the owner asked for, held under commercial
pressure: attraction compounds, coercion decays — and over ten years, compounding
is the better business.
